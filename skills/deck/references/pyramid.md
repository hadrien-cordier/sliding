# The pyramid principle, applied at every level of the deck

The pyramid is no pipeline step. The same logic holds at every level: the sentences of a paragraph serve one idea, the paragraph's idea summarises its sentences, a section's idea summarises its paragraphs, and the governing thought of the document summarises the whole (Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*).

## Three grouping rules
1. **An idea summarises the ideas grouped below it.** The upper level states what the lower level proves or explains. "There are two problems" names a category and summarises nothing: the idea must say what the two problems imply.
2. **Ideas in a group are of the same kind** and at the same level of abstraction. A test: one plural noun names them all (reasons, steps, risks, options).
3. **Ideas in a group follow one logical order**, and only one:
   - deductive: premise, premise, conclusion;
   - time: steps of a process, cause-and-effect sequence;
   - structural: parts of a whole, MECE split;
   - degree: from most to least important, for this reader.

## The question-answer dialogue forms the vertical logic
Each idea raises a question in the reader's mind (why? how? which?). The level below answers it, and only it. The horizontal logic is the order, deductive or inductive, of the answers on one level.

## SCQA orders the storyline and stays invisible
- **Situation**: what the reader knows and accepts.
- **Complication**: what changes, threatens or raises a problem.
- **Question**: the one the complication raises; a document has one.
- **Answer**: the governing thought.
A complication that does not raise the question signals a wrong complication or a wrong question.

In this deck system the SCQA decides what the reader needs first and orders the executive summary. It never appears as labels ("Situation:", "Complication:") or as a dedicated context slide.

## Translation into a deck

| Level | Rule |
|---|---|
| Deck | One governing thought, stated in the executive summary title. Slide titles read in sequence tell the story (horizontal logic). |
| Executive summary | Written last. Its bold takeaways read alone give the whole argument. |
| Key line (group of slides) | It answers one reader question. Key line items share one kind, do not overlap, follow one order, and together prove the governing thought. |
| Slide | One idea, carried whole by the title. The body answers the question the title raises (vertical logic). The body covers **every** element of the title and **nothing else**. No idea returns from one slide to another. |
| Block, chart | It answers a question raised by the slide title. Its title states its idea; its content details it. Blocks on a slide share one kind and one order. |
| Sentence | One idea per sentence. |

## Failure signals
- A slide title nobody could refute: it names a topic.
- A group of blocks no common idea summarises: a list without links.
- A slide you can remove without changing the story of the titles: it carries no message.
- Two slides whose titles you can swap without loss: overlap.
