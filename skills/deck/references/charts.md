# Rules for charts and colour

## Choose the chart
- Reading accuracy (Cleveland and McGill, 1984): position on a common scale > position on non-aligned scales > length > angle and slope > area. Prefer bars to pies and bubbles.
- Vertical bars for a wide format, horizontal bars for long labels or a tall format.
- Line for a continuous trend; it may start above zero. Bars always start at zero (Datawrapper).

## Draw it
- The chart title states what the data shows, not what it measures.
- Direct labels on the data rather than a legend or a graduated axis; label precision matched to the gaps shown (think-cell).
- Bright colour for the key data, grey for the rest: highlighting one point hides the others (Knaflic, *Storytelling with Data*).
- Remove the superfluous (Tufte, data-ink); only ornaments that hinder reading must go (Bateman et al., 2010).
- No frame around the chart, no gridlines.

## Colour and contrast
- Black for titles, grey for context, axes and sources, one accent colour for the key data.
- Seven categorical colours at most; four if the palette must stay readable by colour-blind readers (ColorBrewer). Avoid red against green.
- WCAG contrast: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold).

## Reading the slide
- The eye moves from top left to bottom right; a strong size gap reverses that order.
- Font hierarchy: title > annotations > data labels > axis labels > sources.
- Place each text next to the visual it comments on (Mayer).

## What no reliable source fixes
Minimum font size, share of slide area for a chart, the 60-30-10 rule: these thresholds are the author's choice, not a standard.
