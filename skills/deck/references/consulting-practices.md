# Consulting deck practices (McKinsey, Bain, BCG, Big 4)

## A note on the reliability of these sources
Consulting firms do not publish their internal storyline and slide manuals. Minto's *The Pyramid Principle* is the one primary text, written by a former McKinsey consultant who trained the firm's staff. Everything else below comes from practitioner accounts by former consultants or from slide-design publishers. The guidance converges across them, which is why it is used here; treat it as common practice, not as any firm's official rule.

## Process: storyline before slides
- **Distil the core message first.** Write the one takeaway the audience must remember before building anything (ThinkInsights, Storyboard).
- **Write the storyline as a dot-dash.** Dots carry the main ideas, dashes the supporting facts and exhibits; this lays out the full narrative before any slide exists (ThinkInsights, Storyboard).
- **Sketch one tagline per slide, on paper, and rearrange freely** until the flow works (ThinkInsights, Storyboard). Draft titles in sequence before building slides (ThinkInsights, Write good slide titles).
- **Build a ghost deck early and test it with stakeholders.** Early feedback aligns everyone on the structure and limits rework (ThinkInsights, Storyboard).
- **Start from a hypothesis and revise.** Develop the storyline early, even though analysis may prove some initial guesses wrong; keep versions (ThinkInsights, Storyboard).
- **Order ideas by time, structure or degree** (ThinkInsights, Storyboard), consistent with Minto.

## Titles
- A title stands alone: a reader unfamiliar with the topic grasps the message without the presenter (ThinkInsights, Write good slide titles).
- Read only the titles in sequence: they form the narrative (ThinkInsights; Deckary, Consulting slide standards).
- Answer the "so what" and take a point of view; a descriptive title leaves the reader to conclude alone (ThinkInsights).
- Keep length, syntax, tense and punctuation consistent across titles (ThinkInsights).
- Practitioners cap titles at about 15 words and two lines, in active voice, as a specific quantified conclusion; a title that needs "and" for two ideas becomes two slides (Deckary, Consulting slide standards). This system aims for two lines and keeps one action verb (see `logical-writing.md`).

## Slide body
- One message per slide, proven by one exhibit or content block; 2 to 4 supporting points, then evidence (Deckary, Consulting slide standards).
- Cut any data or text that does not support the title; remove decoration, clip art and 3D charts (Deckary).
- A slide that cannot support a strong title merges with another, moves to the appendix, or goes (ThinkInsights).
- Source every quantitative claim in a footer source line, with the year of the data; number every page (Deckary).
- Two font families at most, 3 to 4 colours, accent colour for key data only, everything on a grid with fixed title position (Deckary).
- Each slide should be explainable in about one minute (Deckary).
- The appendix holds backup for anticipated questions (Deckary).

## Executive summary
- Written last, after the analysis, so it reflects the actual conclusions (Deckary, Executive summary slides; StrategyU, Slide layouts).
- A complete, standalone version of the argument that a senior executive can read without the other slides; conclusions, never an agenda (Deckary, Executive summary slides).
- Bold lead sentences carry the key claims, with evidence bullets beneath; reading the bold sentences alone must tell a coherent story (Deckary, Executive summary slides).
- 3 to 4 key points on one slide, with specific actions, numbers and timing; no charts or methodology (Deckary, Executive summary slides).
- Practitioner accounts of Big 4 decks describe an executive summary built for senior stakeholders, holding findings, recommendations and next steps (SlideModel, Deloitte presentation structure; not authored by Deloitte).

## Deliberate departures in this system
- **No agenda, background or methodology slides**, although practitioner accounts of Big 4 decks list them (SlideModel). The author's rule: every slide carries a message for the reader.
- **SCQA stays implicit.** Some executive summaries show Situation, Complication and Resolution as visible sections (Deckary). Here the SCQA orders the takeaways without labels.

## Standard consulting slide designs
Practitioner catalogues list the same recurring designs (StrategyU, Slide layouts; Deckary, Consulting slide templates): big number, column chart, chart with insight callout, two-column comparison, three pillars, process flow, waterfall, funnel, 2x2 matrix, Harvey ball scorecard, quote with evidence, timeline, Gantt or roadmap, dashboard, issue tree, executive summary, recommendations. `layouts.md` maps each to the logical relation it shows and to the generator.
