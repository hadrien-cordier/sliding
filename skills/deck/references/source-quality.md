# Source quality and verification

A deck loses its reader at the first wrong figure. Every claim that reaches a slide must be traceable to who produced it and confirmed by someone who did not copy it.

## Source tiers

| Tier | What it covers | Use on a slide |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Producer of the data: regulators and supervisors, central banks, official statistics offices, legislation and official journals, company filings and annual reports, standards bodies, peer-reviewed studies, the study's own authors | Alone, once traced |
| 2 | Reputable relays with a stated method: major news agencies and financial press, research firms that publish their methodology, international organisations' syntheses, industry associations | With a second independent source |
| 3 | Blogs, vendor marketing, content farms, aggregators, uncited statistics pages, AI-generated summaries, forums, social media | Never on a slide; use only to find a better source |

A vendor's figure about its own market or product stays an opinion, whatever its tier.

## Method: trace, read laterally, confirm
Adapted from the SIFT approach to verification (stop, investigate the source, find better coverage, trace claims to the original context) and lateral reading.

1. **Trace to the origin.** Follow each figure back through every relay to the organisation that produced it. Cite the origin, never the relay.
2. **Investigate the source.** Leave its page and search what independent outlets say about it: who runs it, who funds it, what interest it has in the claim (Princeton University Library; OpenClassrooms).
3. **Find better coverage.** Check whether serious, independent outlets carry the claim. A claim that only its original page makes is a warning (OpenClassrooms).
4. **Match the claim to the origin**: same number, unit, scope, population, date and definition.
5. **Check freshness**: take the latest figure from the same origin.
6. **Log every check**: what you searched, what you found, your conclusion (OpenClassrooms).

## Verdicts
- **CONFIRMED**: origin reached and tier 1, or two independent tier 2 sources agree; scope and date match.
- **CORRECTED**: the origin gives another number, scope or date; use the origin's version.
- **REJECTED**: no reachable origin, unresolved disagreement, or tier 3 only.
- **User document**: a claim from an internal document the user vouches for, marked as such.

## Red flags
- A round figure with no method.
- The same figure in many articles that all cite each other, or none.
- A figure without a date, or older than the decision it supports.
- A scope shift: global figure used for one country, sector figure used for one firm.
- A ratio or total computed by the writer, presented as data.
